Does your debt make you money?
Not all debt is equal. The question is: does it put money in your pocket or take it out?
Bad debt — takes money out

Credit cards, personal loans, buy-now-pay-later, car loans on a car you only drive. This debt costs you money every single month and the thing you bought is losing value. A $5,000 credit card at 24% APR costs $1,200/year in interest alone.

Good debt — puts money in

A mortgage on a rental property that earns rent. A business loan for a business that generates income. An investment loan that produces returns higher than the interest. The debt pays for itself and then some.

This is your priority

Consumer debt, car loans, and buy-now-pay-later are the most dangerous. They carry the highest interest rates (20-30%), and the things you bought with them are losing value every day. The Quiet Abundance method locks your focus here first. No savings goals, no investments, until this weight is lifted.

Good debt can wait. Bad debt cannot.

Good debt is a tool, not a burden

Your income-producing debt is working for you. A rental mortgage that earns rent, a business loan that generates revenue — these are leveraged investments. Focus on making sure the income always exceeds the repayments.

Understanding your interest

Credit cards typically charge 20-30% APR. Personal loans 8-15%. Car loans 5-10%. The higher the rate, the more urgently that debt needs to go. We attack the highest-rate debt first (avalanche method) while making minimums on everything else.
